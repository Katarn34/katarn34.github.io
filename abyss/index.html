Project Abyss.

A 10–15 minute underwater tech demo. You play a xenobiologist studying the wildlife of a small cove, with no weapons: you progress by understanding creatures, scanning them and observing their behaviours, down to an immense presence glimpsed in the dark.

What I’m building

Curiosity“I want to get closer.”
Believable 3D steering, readable idle behaviours, a creature that comes to the player.
Tension“It warns before acting.”
A predator with progressive vigilance, explicit stages and telegraphed charges, all in data.
Readability“I always understand.”
Every observable behaviour emits a signal (posture, light, sound), with debug tools to check it.
013D navigation without NavMeshContinues Flying AI
Idea
Unreal’s NavMesh only covers walkable surfaces, so creatures navigate in three layers: a Behavior Tree or State Tree decides what to do, an EQS query picks the next free waypoint towards that goal, and a steering component makes the creature swim there smoothly, banking in turns and chaining waypoints without stopping.
Why
EQS alone plans well but zigzags and stops at each point; steering alone is short-sighted and gets stuck in U-shaped rocks. It builds directly on my Flying AI.
02The Creature TunerEditor Utility Widget
Goal
A Designer picks a species, tunes it, spawns it in play and watches its perception and vigilance live, without opening a Blueprint graph. Target: a new species in under 3 minutes.
How
The editor widget and the play session live in two different worlds, so a Data Asset serves as the channel between them, through a bridge actor that only exists in non-shipping builds.
03The right framework for each creatureBT · State Tree
Choice
A fish shoal with simple states (cruise, flee, regroup) runs on a State Tree; the sea serpent, with priorities and interruptions, runs on a Behavior Tree. Part of the goal is to show I work inside Unreal’s frameworks instead of reinventing them.
